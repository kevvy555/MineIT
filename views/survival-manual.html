MINEIT FIELD MANUAL

MineIT is a survival-focused mining-corporation game. Build each colony from local resources, keep Food and Power stable, develop Industry and export valuable material to fund the corporation.

Rules current through v5.6.4. Local construction uses colony resources; corporation cash is reserved for external purchases, transport, licences and contract fees.

1. Objective & game loop

Choose one of eight possible landing locations, establish the colony on its fixed 8×8 land area, survey sectors, develop Food/Fuel/Ore/Build production and construct Housing and Industry on suitable surveyed land.

Keep Food and Power stable, grow population deliberately, expand effective Industry and export valuable surplus stock when the corporate ship arrives.

Cash and Power/Food/Mining technology belong to the corporation and are shared across every colony. Population, inventory, land, developments, sites, contract age and survival conditions belong to the individual colony.

Local construction does not consume corporation cash. Cash represents off-world purchasing power and is spent only on external goods, services, licences, transport and contract fees.

2. Survival, shortages & colony death

Food and Power are lethal needs. Around 90%+ effective Food/Power supply is stable. Below 90% the colony becomes strained; below 70% deaths begin; below 40% mortality accelerates sharply.

Stored Food and Fuel provide reaction time. The Colony screen shows supply percentages and estimated net days remaining. Fuel shortages reduce available Power, so a Fuel failure can become a life-support failure.

Population can reach zero. A dead colony permanently loses extraction, Industry and its mining charter and corporation reputation is reduced. There is no generic daily cash operating charge to stop—the loss is primarily people, production, resources and contract progress.

If another colony survives the corporation continues. Losing every colony ends the corporation.

3. Population expansion & transport

Natural population growth is intentionally slow. Meaningful expansion normally comes from importing colonists.

A docked corporate trade ship can transfer colonists using its passenger capacity. The transfer consumes corporation cash and requires enough supported Housing and Power capacity.

Dedicated colony transports can also be ordered without waiting for the normal trade ship. They take {{DEDICATED_TRANSPORT_DAYS}} days and cost a base {{DEDICATED_TRANSPORT_BASE_COST}} plus a per-colonist transport charge modified by environmental support load.

Check Food/Fuel runway, Housing, Power and workforce needs before adding population: new colonists increase survival demand immediately.

4. Industry, workforce & site capacity

Industry needs population, Power and Ore. Each Industry level has a population requirement for full effectiveness, so building ahead of population can leave the industrial base understaffed.

Developed extraction sites also consume Industrial Capacity. Food and Fuel receive priority; if capacity is overloaded, Build and Ore extraction are throttled rather than hard-blocked.

Extraction sites require operational workers. Food/Fuel worker demand is prioritised; commercial Build/Ore sites are reduced if the colony cannot staff everything.

Effective Industry adds processing value to exports, up to +50%, and stronger Industry is required for advanced site upgrades.

5. Screen, time & controls

Top: contract Food Production, Industry and Population objectives.

Map toolbar: switch between LAND and RESOURCES. Resource view also exposes filters.

Map: tap an unsurveyed tile to queue a survey, hold/drag across unsurveyed tiles to queue several, tap a revealed tile to act, and hold a tile for detailed controls.

Footer: corporation Cash and Stock Value, then current-colony Food, Build, Fuel and Ore stock.

COLLECT: active extraction. SHIP: exports, imports and ship transfers. COLONY: survival and infrastructure. COLONIES: portfolio. TECH: permanent corporation licences.

6. Surveying & discovery

Surveying costs time, not cash. It reveals the resource, exact quality, finite deposit size or renewable abundance, and the Mining technology required to exploit it.

Distance and world scan difficulty affect survey duration. Mining technology reduces scan time, improves hints and gradually raises concurrent survey slots from one to five.

Surveying is disabled once mining rights have ended and on dead colonies.

7. Land, resource view & filters

The colony land area is a fixed 8×8 grid. LAND view emphasises terrain and constructed developments; RESOURCES view emphasises surveyed deposits and extraction state.

Resource filtering has four independent groups: State (Developed / Not Developed / Locked), Type (Food / Build / Fuel / Ore), Size and the six Quality bands. Each group has ALL and CLEAR controls.

A resource tile must pass every active filter group to remain visible. Unsurveyed sectors and the colony ship are kept visible so filtering never hides navigation.

8. Resource catalogue

The four categories serve different roles. Food keeps colonists alive. Fuel supports Power. Ore feeds Industry. Build is the physical construction stock used for Housing, Industry and extraction development.

Build is deliberately a local material cost, not corporation cash. Cash is earned mainly by exporting stock and is reserved for external transactions.

9. Quality, production & sale value

Natural resources have Q1–Q10,000. Quality affects economic value, not the basic collection rate. This means a low-quality and high-quality deposit of the same size and site level can produce similar tonnage, but the high-quality material is worth much more when exported.

{{QUALITY_ROWS}}

Collection rate is driven primarily by site level, deposit/renewable size, terrain yield and current workforce/Industry throttling. Quarry/Mine/Deep Mine/Rig overdrive can temporarily raise finite-site output further.

Inventory is stored in quality bands and each band sells at its own multiplier. Colony consumption uses available stock regardless of export value, so exporting every high-value resource can still create local shortages.

10. Finite deposits & renewable sites

Finite deposits are Small, Modest, Large, Huge or Colossal. Larger deposits are easier to develop and have better collection-rate factors. Extraction reduces the remaining reserve until the deposit is exhausted.

Renewable Food sites plus renewable Biomass and Construction Fibre use the existing harvest-intensity system. Their abundance can be Limited, Established, Large or Vast.

Renewable does not mean impossible to destroy. Harvesting above the sustainable level gradually damages the site; continued over-harvesting can downgrade abundance and eventually wipe the resource out. Harvesting below the sustainable level allows recovery.

Food keeps this renewable harvest model; the newer industrial overdrive/accident system does not replace it.

11. Local construction, site upgrades & overdrive

Housing, Industry and extraction construction do not spend corporation cash. Local building consumes Build material and must satisfy terrain, technology, capacity and other relevant requirements. Extraction development also needs enough free operational workforce.

Extraction sites require the appropriate Mining technology. Advanced upgrades need higher Mining and Industry levels plus additional Build and workforce. Base L1–L5 site output is {{SITE_OUTPUT_LEVELS}} units/day before deposit-size, terrain, staffing, Industry and operating-mode effects.

Finite Quarry, Mine, Deep Mine and Rig sites can run NORMAL, PUSHED or HARD. NORMAL uses 100% workforce/output and reduces accumulated risk. PUSHED uses 125% workforce for 115% output and adds 0.3 risk-days/day. HARD uses 150% workforce for 130% output and adds 1 risk-day/day.

Every 30 accumulated risk-days triggers a 25% accident check. Accidents are normally machinery damage (75%) but can cause fatalities (25%). Quarry Face Collapse, Tunnel Collapse, Rockburst & Shaft Collapse and Over-pressure Blowout are the current facility incidents.

Any accident shuts that facility for 3 full game days, stops its production/workforce use, clears accumulated risk and resets it to NORMAL before reopening.

12. Emergency Mode

Emergency Mode is a survival control. It shuts down Industry and Build/Ore extraction while keeping Food and Fuel sites prioritised, and it reduces life-support Power demand.

Use it when Food or Fuel runway is collapsing or population growth has outpaced support. It no longer exists to reduce a generic cash operating charge—there is no such daily cash drain under the current economy.

The trade-off remains severe: Industry becomes zero and non-essential extraction stops, so leaving Emergency Mode on will prevent normal contract and economic progress.

13. Environmental support load

World type has a real support multiplier, ranging from relatively easy Verdant/Temperate worlds to severe Deep Reach environments.

Support load increases life-support Power demand and the cost of importing/transporting colonists. Harder worlds therefore need stronger Fuel/Power systems and more external transport cash for the same population.

Support load does not create a generic daily cash operating bill. Local survival is paid for with resources and infrastructure.

14. Corporation technology

Power, Food Production and Mining licences are permanent corporation-wide upgrades and remain major cash purchases.

Power raises generation, population and Industry caps while reducing Fuel intensity. Food Production improves Food-workforce efficiency and unlocks increasing synthetic Food on advanced worlds. Mining unlocks deposits, reduces mining workforce requirements, improves surveying and determines how far extraction sites can be upgraded.

Technology is one of the main intended uses of corporation cash because the licence/knowledge comes from outside the colony.

15. Corporate ship, exports, imports & cash

The corporate ship normally arrives every {{TRADE_INTERVAL_DAYS}} days and pauses corporation time while its visit is unresolved. Export stock, import emergency resources, transfer colonists and—where required—purchase technology before departure.

Cash represents off-world purchasing power. It is spent on technology licences, imported resources, ship/dedicated colonist transport, contract extensions, renewals and population relocation. Local Housing, Industry, extraction construction/upgrades and normal daily colony operation do not consume cash.

Imported resources cost more than their export value and consume finite import cargo. Exports are quality-banded and receive the colony's Industry processing bonus. The first colony starts from corporation cash with no contract advance; later colony contracts can provide an establishment advance.

16. Contracts, goals & scoring

The initial charter lasts 10 years. Goals use current local Food production, effective Industry and population. Contract profit is local export revenue minus tracked external cash costs charged to that colony.

Bronze requires all objectives. Silver, Gold and Platinum add increasing profit and over-performance thresholds. Missing the deadline allows up to {{MAX_EXTENSIONS}} paid one-year extensions; exhausting them ends mining rights and turns the colony into a liability.

After successful completion you can renew in {{RENEWAL_YEARS}}-year blocks, remain temporarily in holdover, open another colony or attempt to return the world. Holdover currently has no generic daily corporate cash surcharge; survival resources continue to matter.

17. Multiple colonies & synchronized time

Every colony has independent population, inventory, sites, support load, contract age and survival condition, while corporation Cash and Technology are shared.

All colonies use the same corporation clock. Switching maps does not freeze other worlds: active colonies continue producing and consuming resources in the background, and corporate ship arrivals from any colony interrupt time for resolution.

There is no generic background cash drain, but ignored colonies can still run out of Food/Fuel, lose Industry effectiveness or die. Portfolio warnings and runway values therefore matter.

18. Returns, liability colonies & death

After a successful contract, the corporation may accept a colony back if sufficient surveyed resource potential remains. A heavily depleted world can be refused.

If mining rights end, extraction stops but the population remains your responsibility. Food, Fuel, Power and other support requirements continue. A liability colony does not incur an arbitrary higher daily cash operating charge.

If another colony exists, the corporation can pay the explicit external relocation cost to move the population and close the liability. Otherwise life-support failure can still kill the colony.

19. Saving, reset & diagnostics

The game saves to browser storage and periodically auto-saves. Save Now forces an immediate save. Colony maps, portfolio state, resources, site operating modes/risk, accident shutdowns, transports and survival stocks persist.

Diagnostics records startup/runtime errors and simulation state. Hard Reset permanently erases MineIT data on this browser and creates a fresh Contract 01 corporation.
