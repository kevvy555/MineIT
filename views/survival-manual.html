MINEIT FIELD MANUAL

MineIT is a survival-focused mining-corporation game. Build each colony from local resources, keep Food and Power stable, develop Industry and export valuable material to fund the corporation.

Rules current through v5.6.4. Local construction uses colony resources; corporation cash is reserved for external purchases, transport, licences and contract fees.

1. Objective & game loop

Choose one of eight possible landing locations, establish the colony on its fixed 8×8 land area, survey sectors, develop Food/Fuel/Ore/Build production and construct Housing and Industry on suitable surveyed land.

Keep Food and Power stable, grow population deliberately, expand effective Industry and export valuable surplus stock when the corporate ship arrives.

Cash and Power/Food/Mining technology belong to the corporation and are shared across every colony. Population, inventory, land, developments, sites, contract age and survival conditions belong to the individual colony.

Local construction does not consume corporation cash. Cash represents off-world purchasing power and is spent only on external goods, services, licences, transport and contract fees.

2. Survival, shortages & colony death

Food and life-support Power are lethal needs for planetary residents. Headquarters receives Power first, followed by Housing and resident life support, survival production, Spaceport services, then commercial Industry and extraction. Facilities within an ordinary priority band share shortages proportionally.

Residents assigned to a docked ship consume that ship's Food and do not use planetary Housing, Power or workforce. Planetary residents use colony Food and life-support Power; authorised emergency ship Food remains a separate last-resort action.

Every online Power Plant burns Fuel for its full generation capacity. Stored Fuel at the beginning of the day determines available generation; Fuel produced during that day becomes available on the next day.

Population can reach zero. A dead colony permanently loses extraction, Industry and its mining charter and corporation reputation is reduced. If another colony survives the corporation continues; losing every colony ends the corporation.

3. Population expansion & transport

Natural population growth is intentionally slow. Meaningful expansion normally comes from importing colonists.

A docked corporate trade ship can transfer colonists using its passenger capacity. The transfer consumes corporation cash and requires enough supported Housing and Power capacity.

Dedicated colony transports can also be ordered without waiting for the normal trade ship. They take {{DEDICATED_TRANSPORT_DAYS}} days and cost a base {{DEDICATED_TRANSPORT_BASE_COST}} plus a per-colonist transport charge modified by environmental support load.

Check Food/Fuel runway, Housing, Power and workforce needs before adding population: new colonists increase survival demand immediately.

4. Headquarters, Industry & Power

Housing, Power and Industry are individual L1–L5 map buildings; Headquarters use the same L1–L5 construction model. Multiple buildings stack. The founding ship supplies no colony Power and supplies 50 Industry only while docked at the colony it founded.

Before the founding colony ship can leave for the first time, a fully constructed and fully staffed Headquarters must be selected as Primary. Headquarters provide command capacity; overload reduces extraction, processing and surveying efficiency. A crewed command-capable ship can manage temporarily when the Primary is non-operational.

After command handover, an unpowered or understaffed Primary Headquarters takes the conglomerate network offline immediately. Existing scheduled commitments continue, but new conglomerate orders are blocked. Colony work begins at 90% efficiency and loses one further percentage point per full offline day, stopping completely at 100% loss.

Restoring the Primary Headquarters restores network access immediately. The actual accumulated efficiency loss then recovers evenly over ten days; emergency ship command does not replace the Primary's conglomerate link.

Industry has a small idle demand plus a larger variable demand based on staffed operational capacity. Food and Fuel facilities receive their required Industry support before commercial Industry and Build/Ore facilities.

Open any Power Plant for online capacity, Fuel-limited generation, demand and delivery by priority band, Fuel burn, unused generation and the exact limiting cause.

5. Screen, global time & controls

Corporation time is global. Pause, ×1, ×2 and ×4 affect every living colony together. The header shows Corporation Year/Day; each colony's contract age is tracked from the global day it was founded.

When a corporate ship reaches any colony, the entire corporation pauses, that colony is brought forward automatically, and the ship must be handled. If several ships arrive on the same day they are processed as a queue.

Map controls never cover the playable canvas: State, Type, Size and Quality filters live above it and each group has ALL/CLEAR controls.

6. Surveying & discovery

Surveying costs time, not cash. It reveals the resource, exact quality, finite deposit size or renewable abundance, and the Mining technology required to exploit it.

Distance and world scan difficulty affect survey duration. Mining technology reduces scan time, improves hints and gradually raises concurrent survey slots from one to five.

Surveying is disabled once mining rights have ended and on dead colonies.

7. Land, resource view & filters

The colony land area is a fixed 8×8 grid. LAND view emphasises terrain and constructed developments; RESOURCES view emphasises surveyed deposits and extraction state.

Resource filtering has four independent groups: State (Developed / Not Developed / Locked), Type (Food / Build / Fuel / Ore), Size and the six Quality bands. Each group has ALL and CLEAR controls.

A resource tile must pass every active filter group to remain visible. Unsurveyed sectors and the colony ship are kept visible so filtering never hides navigation.

8. Resource catalogue

The four categories serve different roles. Food keeps colonists alive. Fuel supports Power. Ore feeds Industry. Build is the physical construction stock used for Housing, Industry and extraction development.

Build is deliberately a local material cost, not corporation cash. Cash is earned mainly by exporting stock and is reserved for external transactions.

9. Quality & sale value

Natural resources still have Q1–Q10,000, but quality no longer changes extraction speed. Quality is an economic property: harvested material is grouped into Common, Good, Excellent, Exceptional, Rare and Extraordinary price bands.

Extraction throughput is determined by site development, deposit/renewable size, available workforce and Industrial Capacity. This prevents one high-Q precious discovery from becoming an instant high-throughput money printer.

10. Deposit size & sustainable resources

Finite deposit size affects both total reserve and how readily a large operation can be developed. Small sites are less productive and somewhat more expensive per unit of capability; Huge/Colossal sites receive a modest throughput and development-cost advantage.

Renewables have Limited, Established, Large or Vast sustainable yield. Set harvest between 25% and 200% in 25% steps. Above 100% the resource is over-farmed and can degrade Vast → Large → Established → Limited → permanently Wiped Out. Below 100%, damaged resources recover slowly up to their original surveyed size.

11. Buildings, extraction levels & overdrive

Local construction uses physical colony resources, not corporation cash. Housing, Power and Industry buildings consume Build and increasingly Ore at advanced levels. Their L1–L5 upgrades require the matching corporation technology level.

Food facilities also use L1–L5 development and require matching Food Production technology. Quarry/Mine/Deep Mine/Rig levels represent development of that specific deposit rather than a technology tier.

Industrial extraction retains NORMAL/PUSHED/HARD overdrive. PUSHED uses 125% staff for 115% output; HARD uses 150% staff for 130% output. Every 30 accumulated risk-days triggers a 25% accident check, and an accident closes the facility for three full game days.

12. Emergency Mode

Emergency Mode is a survival control. It shuts down Industry and Build/Ore extraction while keeping Food and Fuel sites prioritised, and it reduces life-support Power demand.

Use it when Food or Fuel runway is collapsing or population growth has outpaced support. It no longer exists to reduce a generic cash operating charge—there is no such daily cash drain under the current economy.

The trade-off remains severe: Industry becomes zero and non-essential extraction stops, so leaving Emergency Mode on will prevent normal contract and economic progress.

13. Environmental support load

World type has a real support multiplier, ranging from relatively easy Verdant/Temperate worlds to severe Deep Reach environments.

Support load increases life-support Power demand and the cost of importing/transporting colonists. Harder worlds therefore need stronger Fuel/Power systems and more external transport cash for the same population.

Support load does not create a generic daily cash operating bill. Local survival is paid for with resources and infrastructure.

14. Corporation technology

There are five corporation technology families: Housing, Power, Food Production, Industry and Mining/Extraction.

Housing, Power, Food and Industry Tech L1–L5 directly set the maximum level of that building/facility family. You may build any number of unlocked buildings; technology limits sophistication, not quantity.

Mining is the exception. Mining L1–L10 unlocks extraction methods and harder resource types. Once a deposit is unlocked, its Quarry/Mine/Deep Mine/Rig can be developed independently from L1–L5 for greater output. Mine upgrades depend on local Industry, Power, workforce and materials rather than buying an unrelated higher Mining level.

15. Trade ships, export capacity & colonist transport

Regular corporate ships arrive every {{TRADE_INTERVAL_DAYS}} days of the shared corporation calendar. Any colony's arrival pauses the entire corporation and must be dealt with before time resumes.

Sell groups are collapsed by default and sorted by total sale value. Their headers show total quantity and value across every quality band. Imports remain limited. Exports have a deliberately generous {{TRADE_BASE_EXPORT_CARGO}}-unit base capacity, scaling with reputation, so normal stock does not accumulate while extreme windfalls cannot be liquidated without limit in a single visit.

The regular ship still carries {{TRADE_PASSENGER_CAPACITY}} immediate colonists. For serious expansion, order a dedicated colony transport at any time: any supported quantity can be requested subject to Housing, Power and cash, arriving {{DEDICATED_TRANSPORT_DAYS}} days later.

16. Contracts, goals & scoring

The initial charter lasts 10 years. Goals use current local Food production, effective Industry and population. Contract profit is local export revenue minus tracked external cash costs charged to that colony.

Bronze requires all objectives. Silver, Gold and Platinum add increasing profit and over-performance thresholds. Missing the deadline allows up to {{MAX_EXTENSIONS}} paid one-year extensions; exhausting them ends mining rights and turns the colony into a liability.

After successful completion you can renew in {{RENEWAL_YEARS}}-year blocks, remain temporarily in holdover, open another colony or attempt to return the world. Holdover currently has no generic daily corporate cash surcharge; survival resources continue to matter.

17. Multiple colonies & one corporation clock

All colonies share one Corporation Year/Day and one simulation speed. Switching maps never pauses or accelerates an individual colony. Every living colony produces, consumes, surveys, receives transports and faces contract deadlines on the same advancing calendar.

Each contract keeps its own start day and therefore its own age/deadline. A colony founded in Corporation Year 8 still receives its full contract term.

18. Returns, liability colonies & death

After a successful contract, the corporation may accept a colony back if sufficient surveyed resource potential remains. A heavily depleted world can be refused.

If mining rights end, extraction stops but the population remains your responsibility. Food, Fuel, Power and other support requirements continue. A liability colony does not incur an arbitrary higher daily cash operating charge.

If another colony exists, the corporation can pay the explicit external relocation cost to move the population and close the liability. Otherwise life-support failure can still kill the colony.

19. Saving, game log & diagnostics

The game saves the global calendar, all colonies, transport orders, renewable health, trade state and structured game log. Save Now forces a browser save.

GAME LOG records meaningful actions and events such as discoveries, construction, trade, transports, deaths, renewable degradation and contracts. Every {{LOG_TELEMETRY_INTERVAL_DAYS}} corporation days it also records economy/population/site snapshots. Download the full JSON log and upload it for progression/balance analysis.

Diagnostics remains for runtime/startup faults. Hard Reset permanently deletes MineIT browser data.
